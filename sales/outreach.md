# Outreach messages

Keep them short, personal and specific. Only claim what you can show; swap in real results as clients come in. Never tell the story of a specific company you've worked with or for. Change the first line for every person. Never paste the same message to 50 people.

## LinkedIn connection note (300 characters max)
> Hi [Name], I'm an ex-Google operations leader helping companies like [their company] get all their data in one place and put it to work. I'd value connecting, no pitch.

## LinkedIn message after they accept
> Thanks for connecting, [Name]. Quick question, as one operator to another: how does your leadership team get its weekly numbers today? In my experience someone rebuilds it in Excel every Monday, and half the meeting goes on arguing about whose number is right. What fixes it is getting every system into one place, then building the scorecard, tools and AI on top. Happy to show you what that looked like if it's useful. Either way, no pressure.

## Cold email (3 short emails, a week apart)
**Email 1. Subject: your Monday numbers**
> Hi [Name],
>
> How long does it take your team to put together the leadership numbers each week?
>
> We bring a company's systems (CRM, accounting, projects, spreadsheets) into one place, then build on top of it: a weekly scorecard that fills itself in, tools that replace spreadsheets, and AI agents that draft the reports and briefs your team spends hours on. Would a 20-minute call to see if it fits [their company] be worth it?
>
> Noah

**Email 2. Subject: re: your Monday numbers**
> Hi [Name], once a company's data is in one place, the useful part starts: a scorecard nobody has to build, alerts when a big deal slips, and AI that can actually answer questions about the business.
>
> Here's a 2-minute look at a working example: [demo link]. If it's interesting, a short call is the easiest way to see what it would look like for [their company].

**Email 3. Subject: closing the loop**
> Hi [Name], I won't keep emailing. If getting trusted numbers out of your CRM becomes a priority, I'm here. I'm happy to talk whenever the timing's right. Good luck with the rest of the year.

## Referral partners (fractional CFOs, CPAs, IT providers, CRM consultants)
> Hi [Name], We help small and mid-size companies get all their data in one place, with dashboards, tools, AI and AI agents on top. Your clients are exactly the companies that struggle with this, and it usually makes *your* work easier too (cleaner data, fewer reconciliations). Could we grab 20 minutes? I'd like to understand your clients, and I pay a referral fee of 10% of first-year fees for introductions that turn into work.

## After a conversation (same day)
> Thanks for the time today, [Name]. You mentioned [their exact pain]. As promised, here's [the thing you promised]. The next step we discussed: [step], on [date]. Talk then.

## Follow-up when they go quiet (2 weeks later)
> Hi [Name], no rush on this. I just came across [a relevant thing: an article, a stat, a fix] and thought of what you said about [their pain]. Happy to pick the conversation back up whenever the timing's right.

## LinkedIn post ideas (operator voice, 1–2 a week)
1. "What an AI agent can actually do for a COO": a daily brief, a drafted weekly report, meeting prep. No hype.
2. "Never average percentages": why a branch win-rate average lies, with a tiny worked example.
3. "Your CRM's AI only sees your CRM": why Copilot can't answer real business questions yet.
4. "Win rate by count vs by dollars": you can win most deals and still lose most of the money.
5. "The Monday meeting test": if half the meeting is arguing about numbers, the problem isn't the people.
6. "What becomes possible once the data is in one place": the tools nobody could build while it was scattered.
7. "$6 a month": what it actually costs to run a company's data hub, versus the licences people buy.
8. "Before you buy Copilot": three questions to ask about your data first.
9. "What a self-filling scorecard changed": the habit shift, not the tech.
10. "Why I reproduce the old report to the cent before showing anything new."
