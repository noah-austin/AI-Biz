## Before you start

Do these in order. About an hour in total, then posts go out over four weeks.

1. Profile photo: a real headshot of you, face and shoulders, plain background, good light. People hire people; the seal goes on the company page.
2. Banner, headline, About, Experience and Featured: section 1 below.
3. Create the company page: section 2. LinkedIn asks you to confirm you're allowed to create it; use noah@sanfordoperations.com if it asks for a work email.
4. Add Sanford Operations as your current job, linked to the new page, so the seal shows next to your name.
5. Hold the launch post until sanfordoperations.com and the demo are live (after Monday's server setup). Then follow the schedule in section 3.

### Week 1, Thursday · you: Your CRM’s AI only sees your CRM

Image: `website/brand/linkedin/post-crm-ai.png`

```
Your CRM’s AI only sees your CRM.

That sounds obvious, but it’s why so many AI rollouts disappoint.

The questions leaders actually ask cross systems:
• Which customers are buying more but paying slower?
• Are we winning the jobs that make money, or just the ones that close?
• Which branch is behind on billing, and is it the pipeline or the invoicing?

Answering those takes the CRM, the accounting system and usually a spreadsheet or two. An AI that can see only one of them gives you a confident, partial answer.

Before you roll out more AI, put the data in one place. Then the AI has something real to work with.

#AI #Operations
```

### Week 2, Tuesday · you: Win rate by count vs by dollars

Image: `website/brand/linkedin/post-win-rate.png`

```
You can win most of your deals and still lose most of the money.

A made-up example: 10 deals. You win 7 small ones worth $300K and lose 3 big ones worth $700K.

Win rate by count: 70%.
Win rate by dollars: 30%.

Both are true. They answer different questions. Count tells you how often you close. Dollars tell you whether you’re winning the work that matters.

Most CRM dashboards show one by default. If your team only sees count, they may be celebrating the wrong thing.

We put both on the same page, side by side, for every branch and every salesperson.

#Sales #Operations
```

### Week 2, Thursday · you: The Monday meeting test

Image: `website/brand/linkedin/post-monday.png`

```
The Monday meeting test.

If half the leadership meeting is spent arguing about whose number is right, the problem usually isn’t the people.

It’s that sales pulled their number from the CRM on Friday, finance pulled theirs from accounting on Monday, and someone adjusted a spreadsheet in between. Everyone is right about their own number.

A better agenda won’t help. One set of numbers will: defined once, written down in plain English, and pulled from the same place by everyone.

When the numbers stop being the debate, the meeting gets to talk about the business.

#Leadership #Operations
```

### Week 3, Tuesday · you: What an AI agent can actually do for a COO

```
What can an AI agent actually do for a COO? Not the hype version. The useful version.

Here’s what ours prepare every morning, from the company’s own data:

Two rules make it work. The agent works from data that’s all in one place, so it isn’t guessing. And a person approves anything that goes out.
```

### Week 3, Thursday · you: Before you buy Copilot

```
Before you buy Copilot, or any AI assistant, for the company, ask three questions about your data.

1. Where does it live? If the answer is the CRM, accounting, a project tool and twelve spreadsheets, the AI will see pieces of the picture.

2. Do your numbers agree? If sales and finance report different revenue, the AI will happily quote either one.

3. Who should see what? A branch manager shouldn’t get the company’s payroll because they asked nicely.

None of these are AI problems. They’re data problems, and they’re solvable. Solve them first and the AI you already pay for gets a lot more useful.

#AI #Operations
```

### Week 4, Tuesday · you: Matching the old report to the cent

```
Why we rebuild your old report, to the cent, before showing you anything new.

New dashboards are exciting. But the first question every leader asks is: “Why doesn’t this match the number I had last week?”

If the answer is “it’s more accurate now,” trust is gone, and it’s hard to win back.

So the first thing we build is a copy of the report you already use, from the new data, matched to the cent. Where it doesn’t match, we find out why and write the rule down in plain English.

Then we build what’s new. By then, people trust the numbers, because they watched them match.

#Operations #Finance
```

### Week 4, Thursday · you: Never average percentages

```
Never average percentages.

A made-up example. Branch A wins 9 of 10 deals: 90%. Branch B wins 10 of 100: 10%.

Average the two rates and you get 50%. But the company actually won 19 of 110 deals: about 17%.

The average gives a branch with 10 deals the same vote as one with 100.

The right way: add up the wins, add up the deals, then divide. Every number in a report should be defined once, written down in plain English, and calculated the same way everywhere.

#Operations #Finance
```
